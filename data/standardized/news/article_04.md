# Your guide to the IELTS Writing test

The IELTS Writing test assesses how well you communicate in written English. For a good score, you’ll need to interpret, organise and communicate information using the correct grammar and vocabulary.

## What is the IELTS Writing test?

The IELTS Writing section tests how well you can  communicate in written English. Across two tasks, you will write texts for different audiences and purposes, such as a letter or an information summary, and a formal essay.

Topics are relevant and interesting, and you won’t need specialist knowledge to write about them.

The IELTS Writing test requires you to complete two tasks within **60 minutes.** Since Task 2 is worth more than Task 1, recommended time limits are:

* **Task 1: 20 minutes**
* **Task 2: 40 minutes**

IELTS Writing is assessed using four criteria:

* Task achievement (Task 1) / Task response (Task 2)
* Coherence and cohesion
* Lexical resource
* Grammatical range and accuracy

For more information on how the IELTS Writing section is scored, visit [How is IELTS assessed?](/results/how-is-ielts-assessed)

## How do Academic and General Training Writing sections compare?

The requirements for Task 1 are different in IELTS Academic than in IELTS General Training. This helps us to assess whether you have the required writing skills for your plans.

*counter\_1*

IELTS Writing Task 1

In Task 1 the Academic test requires you to describe the main features of a graph, table, chart or diagram.

For the General Training test, you’ll need to write a letter responding to a situation. For example, a request, explanation, viewpoint or complaint. You need to use the right tone – formal, semi-formal or informal – for the context.

For both IELTS Academic and IELTS General Training, you will need to write at least 150 words within the recommended time of 20 minutes, leaving you 40 minutes for Task 2.

*counter\_2*

IELTS Writing Task 2

Task 2 of both versions of the test involves writing an essay responding to a point of view, argument, or problem. You might need to present and justify an opinion, compare different views, discuss advantages and disadvantages, or propose solutions to a problem. You will need to support your position with examples.

Task 2 tests your ability to develop ideas, construct arguments, and express opinions, so it requires more complex thought. You’ll need to write a minimum of 250 words in a formal tone.

## What skills do I need for IELTS Writing?

### Analysing and interpreting information

You’ll need to understand, balance and analyse views or problems to identify trends, make comparisons, and present data objectively, without giving opinions.

### Summarising and describing information

You’ll need to be able to write in English using a range of vocabulary and grammatical structures. Include an overview sentence on IELTS Academic Task 1 that summarises the main trend or feature of the visual.

### Structuring your written texts

You’ll need to structure your responses to include an introduction, a body and a conclusion, showing that you can organise ideas logically. Use clear paragraphs, logical progression and a range of linking words to aid coherence.

### Presenting a strong argument

In Task 2, you will need to present a clear position, develop your arguments fully, and stay relevant to the topic throughout. The task may involve comparing different views, discussing advantages and disadvantages, or proposing solutions to a problem. Support your position with examples.

### Managing your word count

Don’t exceed 20% over the recommended minimum word count. You want to write enough to showcase your skills, but don’t include irrelevant information that doesn’t address the question.

### Managing your time

You will need to manage how much time you spend on each task, giving yourself enough time to do both well. Save time to transfer your answers to your answer sheet and to check your responses for mistakes at the end.
